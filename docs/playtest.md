# Playtest brief (19.3)

The last check before the itch.io launch (20.0): can a new player find their feet, and does a term feel fair? This
brief is for whoever runs a session and for the testers themselves. Every tester answers the same questions, so the
answers line up.

## The build

- **Where:** https://nationalpanda17.github.io/forge-command/ (the latest merged version; the version shows on the
  title screen).
- **What it needs:** a desktop browser, a window at least 1100 pixels wide, and sound if the tester likes it. No
  account, nothing to install.
- **Saves** stay in the tester's browser. A tester who wants a fresh start uses New game; nothing needs clearing.

## Who

Aim for six to eight testers, in two kinds:

- **Strategy players** who have not seen the game: they test whether its depth reads as depth or as clutter.
- **People who rarely play strategy games:** they test whether the tutorial and the advisor carry someone who knows
  nothing.

Nobody who has helped build it. They already know where everything is.

## The sessions

Each tester plays one session. Assign them so each kind of tester covers each session at least once.

| Session | Set-up | Length | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Cold start** | New game, Tutorial | 30–45 minutes | The first screens, the tutorial, the first decisions without help |
| **B. One term** | New game, single term, Classic Aldmere, Normal | 60–90 minutes, any speed | A whole term: pacing, the Board, the hearing, the end-of-term report |
| **C. Career** | New game, Career, any posting that is open | 60 minutes, then stop | The career screen, a generated country, the start of a second term if reached |

Tell the tester only the set-up. Do not explain the game, and do not answer "what should I do?". Say "do what you
think is right", and note the question.

## What to watch (for whoever runs a session)

Note the minute and the screen for each of these:

1. **Where they look first** when the map opens, and how long until their first action.
2. **Every time they ask a question aloud** or stop for more than ten seconds with nothing happening.
3. **Whether they find the advisor's "Next step"** on Home, and whether they use "Do it" or do it by hand.
4. **The first Board decision:** do they read the options, take the advised one, or let it default?
5. **The budget hearing** (session B, around the end of the first year): do they understand promises?
6. **Anything that looks broken:** text over text, a button that does nothing, a number that makes no sense.
   Take a screenshot.
7. **What they say when the term ends**, before they read the report.

If the tester is on their own, ask them to think aloud and record their screen if they are happy to.

## The questions

At the end of the session, give the tester these questions to answer in writing:

1. How clear was it what to do next? (1 lost, to 5 always clear)
2. How fair did the game feel? (1 unfair, to 5 fair)
3. How was the pace? (too slow, about right, too fast)
4. Would you play another term? (yes, maybe, no)
5. What confused you, and where?
6. What did you enjoy most?
7. Did anything look broken or wrong?

Ask them to add the game's version (on the title screen), the mode and scenario, and the in-game date they reached,
and to send their answers to whoever runs the playtest, by whatever channel the invitation names.

## Reading the results

- **Clear below 3** from more than one tester, in the same place: an onboarding fix for 19.3, before launch.
- **Fair below 3:** check the report's date and numbers against the balance runs (career and single-term harnesses)
  before changing any tuning. One unlucky seed is not a balance problem.
- **Pace:** "too slow" in session A points at the tutorial; in session B, at the middle of the term.
- **Again "no"** with a reason in question 5 or 7 is the most useful answer here. Follow it up.
- Every "broken" report with a screenshot becomes an issue, fixed before 20.0.

## After

Collect the reports in one place and tally the scales by session and by kind of tester. Fixes go out as 19.3.x
releases; when two sessions in a row raise nothing new, the game is ready for 20.0.
